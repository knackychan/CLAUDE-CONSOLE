Design read

Reading this as: a look back, opened a few times a day from the spend strip, read as one month figure, then the calendar, then one day; dial ENERGY 2 / RHYTHM 2 / MOTION 0.

  • Same tokens, type and cells as the locked Cells Drawer. No new colour: tone steps of ink, orange only for today and the limit, as on the spend strip
  • One Doto figure: the month total. Every other figure is Cascadia Mono in fixed columns
  • No motion: nothing here is live enough to blink

Reading order

  • Head: the month's estimated total, the month with ‹ ›, then tokens, days used and the share of cache reads
  • Four figures in fixed columns: today, this week (Monday first), the average per day used, days over the $15 limit
  • Calendar: one cell per day, Monday first, the cost in whole dollars in the corner. Tone in four steps of the daily limit (under $15, under 3x, under 6x, 6x and more), today ringed in orange like the 80 % mark of the spend strip. Days before the first transcript are outlined, not filled
  • The picked day (today by default; click a day or a Days column): cost, tokens in four columns, each project as 30 cells against that day's top project, the model shares in words as in the spend breakdown
  • Over time: Days (last 30), Weeks (last 12), Months (last 12) as tabs; $ or tokens as a two-button seg. One series, one axis

The chart in cells

  • Each column is one day, week or month, stacked from cells of a fixed amount, like every other quantity in the window. The amount is the smallest of 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50 ... that keeps the tallest column within 20 cells, and is named under the chart ("1 cell = $20")
  • The top cell of a column is half tone when it is only part full, so a $3 day still shows one cell
  • Days in $: the $15 limit is a dashed orange line, named at the left end, where the oldest days sit low. Weeks and months have no limit line
  • Hover or focus a column for its date, cost and tokens; a Days column also picks that day; a Months column moves the calendar to that month
  • Columns before the first transcript are faint ruled stripes, not empty: "no data" never reads as "$0"

What the real data says

  • Spend runs well over the $15 soft limit: 9 of the 11 October days, $78 a day on average, $248 on 10 Oct. That is why the calendar tones step in multiples of the limit and not up to it
  • Cache reads are about 97 % of all tokens (566M of 581M on 10 Oct). Token figures count all four kinds; the picked day splits them
  • Reading every transcript (308 files, 1.42 GB) took 2.2 s on this machine, warm. The service can read them all once at start, in its own thread, then keep following only what grows
